# Guide for professionals and families

This guide is intended for occupational therapists, families,
teachers, public-administration staff and other professionals who
want to use Sinonimia as a support tool to explain difficult words.

---

## Who is Sinonimia for?

Sinonimia is for **anyone who runs into a difficult word** in a
piece of paperwork, an official letter, or a doctor's appointment.
It is not aimed at a clinical audience: the public wording never
names one, because first-read comprehension is the goal for
everyone.

It is especially useful for:

- **People with intellectual disability** who need vocabulary
  support to read official documents.
- **Families and support workers** who explain paperwork at home.
- **Front-desk staff** in public administration, social services,
  health centres, banks, etc., who want to point someone to a clear
  explanation of a term that just came up.

---

## How to use Sinonimia in support sessions

### Before the session

1. **Identify the document** the person is reading (letter from the
   tax office, social security notification, doctor's report…).
2. **Circle or copy the words** that look hard. There is usually no
   need to translate the whole document; the dictionary is best used
   for individual words.
3. **Open Sinonimia in the language the person reads in**. The
   reader's language is more important than the document's: if the
   person reads better in English than in Spanish, the English
   dictionary is the right tool.

### During the session

- **Type the word in the search box**. Sinonimia accepts close
  spellings (singular/plural, with/without accent) and points at the
  closest match.
- **Read the definition aloud** the first time, then let the person
  re-read it.
- **Use the example sentence** as the second step. If the example is
  still abstract, the reader can write their own example in **Mis
  frases** ("My sentences"). It is saved locally in the browser and
  never leaves the device.
- **Filter by topic** (administración, justicia, salud…) when the
  person wants to browse a whole domain, not a single word.

### Optional: use the games to consolidate

Sinonimia ships two light games (Match and Order, see
[`activities.md`](activities.md) §2). They are designed for short
sessions (5–10 questions) and are not an exam. Recommended use:

- Play after the dictionary reading to lock the word in.
- Use the **Match** game to consolidate word ↔ definition.
- Use the **Order** game to practice reading the example.

---

## Privacy

- No login, no account, no analytics, no server calls.
- "Mis frases" (the reader's own sentences), the **known-words**
  progress and the game scores live in **the browser's
  `localStorage`**, namespaced per language. They never leave the
  device.
- Different browsers on the same device store independent progress.

---

## When Sinonimia is not enough

Sinonimia is a dictionary, not a legal or medical advice service.
When a word's meaning changes the decision the person is about to
make (e.g. signing a contract, accepting a payment plan, agreeing
to a procedure), route them to a **human** with authority on the
matter: their family, their lawyer, their social worker, their
doctor. The dictionary is the first step, not the last one.
